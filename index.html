Yogendra Limbu

Biography

I am Yogendra Limbu, a condensed matter physicist specializing in ab initio studies of strongly correlated quantum materials, particularly transition-metal and rare-earth based systems. I did my Ph.D. in Physics at the University of Iowa under the guidence of Prof. Michael E. Flatte.

Strongly correlated quantum materials, such as rare-earth (RE) and transition-metal (TM)-based compounds, are at the heart of emerging quantum information science and spintronic technologies. Gaining insight into their functional capabilities requires a detailed understanding of their electronic structure, magnetic properties, and the quantum phenomena arising from their localized 3d and 4f electrons.

RE ions embedded in solid-state hosts have attracted significant interest as promising platforms for quantum information technologies, owing to their localized 4f electronic states, which give rise to sharp optical transitions and long coherence times, making them suitable for quantum networks, quantum memories, and microwave-to-optical transduction. In addition, the electronic spin of RE ions can be optically addressed, enabling the realization of scalable RE-based qubits. A central requirement for these applications is a precise understanding of how the surrounding crystal environment splits the 4f energy levels through crystal-field interactions. Furthermore, RE dopants can also significantly influence the electronic and magnetic properties of the host material due to the presence of different possible stable charge states. A detailed understanding of these effects is essential, since the performance of RE ions in quantum technologies depends not only on their intrinsic atomic properties but also on how the host environment stabilizes specific charge configurations and preserves the localization of 4f electrons. Therefore, we have systematically investigated the electronic structure, magnetic properties, and defect formation energies of RE-doped systems, highlighting the crucial role of the local chemical environment in determining the electronic structure and in subtly tuning crystal-field energy levels.
